# Panel — the-parser-reads-the-field-plot-writes

**Reconciled: `unanimous` — amend (premise, blast-radius)**

Both jurors confirm the defect and the inversion. Both found the plan's evidence
weaker than its diagnosis.

## The gate that proves the fix, and the one that proves nothing

`premise` named the strong clause and said to keep it exactly:

> the reporter's sequence works end to end — approve a both-shapes plan, then
> `plot-fleet-scan.sh` answers `eligible=1`, **pinned by a test that performs
> the approval rather than by editing a fixture**

It runs the real writer, then an independent reader, and asserts an integer that
is `0` today. **The parenthetical is what closes the loophole.**

`blast-radius` measured why the other headline clause proves nothing: **zero
plans on this estate carry both shapes.** *"All 292 parse byte-identically"* is
satisfied by doing nothing, and cannot see the defect.

## One test breaks, and it is the contract being inverted

```
✖ parser: front matter design: outranks a ## Status Design: line
ℹ tests 97   pass 96   fail 1
```

`parser.test.mjs:582` is **the only both-shapes artifact in the repository**, and
its comment states the rule this plan reverses. The plan claimed byte-identical
behaviour and named no test to change — an implementer meeting the letter of the
`Done when` would hit a red suite with no instruction.

**And the distinction inside it matters**: only `format` moves. `design_raw`
stays front-matter-wins, because no lifecycle script writes a `Design:` field.
**The precedence moves for the fields Plot owns, not for every field** — a
sentence the plan did not have and now does.

## What the lenses had in common

**Both read the parser and neither asked what a plan SHOULD carry.** The fix
makes a both-shapes plan readable; it does not ask why adopters' plans carry
front matter at all, or whether `/plot-idea`'s template should stop emitting a
shape no writer maintains. That is the question upstream of this defect, and no
juror raised it.

## The moderator's reading

**Unanimous, and the amendments are about evidence rather than design.** The
inversion survives; what changed is that the plan now names the test it breaks,
says which field keeps its old precedence, and admits its estate-wide gate is a
regression lock and not proof.

**Nothing here moves the plan's phase.**
