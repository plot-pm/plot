## Reasoning

Three of the plan's claims are wrong in ways that do not depend on the cleared estate, and one of them removes the plan's reason to exist.

**The recognition-and-silence premise is false.** The plan's motivation is that an unplaced desk is "not reaped, not kept, not counted and not named". I created one and both readers named it: the reaper printed an `unknown` row and incremented `unplaced=1`, and the reconcile scan's §21 printed an `unclassified-tree` finding carrying no removal command. The plan cites `plot-reap.sh:415` for a fall-through that the code has not done since PR #878 on 2026-09-10 — the very commit whose header sentence the plan quotes as the historical precedent. The plan read the header's account of the **old** defect and reported it as current. The counter added to fix it sits nine lines below the comment it cited.

**Slice 1 therefore proposes work that exists.** Every element of its Done-when line is present: the finding names the tree, it is under the configured root, it carries no removal command, and `desks=` is the machine-countable footer key. The only element genuinely missing is the **commit count** per tree — a real but much smaller gap than the slice describes, and one that does not need a new finding, only a field on an existing one.

**Slice 2's causal table is inverted.** The plan says `--start` writes the pid "at creation" and the other paths do not, making the marker a creator property. It is not: no creation path writes it. The wrapper writes it when a worker launches. That reframes the open question the plan cannot answer — two `free-*` desks with no pid are most plausibly desks cut but not yet started, which is the normal intermediate state of a free agent, not a lost marker. Slice 2 as written ("every path that creates a desk writes `.plot-worker.pid` at creation") would make a never-started desk indistinguishable from a running one to `plot-worker-state.sh`, which reads that file's pid for liveness — a regression the plan does not consider because its table says the write already happens there.

**The numbers that cannot be rechecked are a separate matter.** 16/6/10, "3 reapable, 4 kept, 9 unplaced", and "three created this morning" are unverifiable: the desks were cleared after 09:42:50 and all five survivors postdate the plan. I do not call those wrong. But two things weaken them. The 9-vs-10 gap is a genuine internal inconsistency — the same reading stated as two numbers in one document, with nothing reconciling them. And `free-0f3f1d2f` / `free-7330dc2a` are the only desk hashes in this estate with zero corroboration outside the file that names them, where comparable hashes appear in one to three panel and log files each. That is not proof of fabrication; it is an absence of the evidence trail every neighbouring measurement left.

**What is left standing.** The board-side symptom is real and untouched by any of this: a worktree with no manifest still synthesizes an agent row, and the operator's "1 manifest, 10 synthesized" reading is a genuine observation. The plan correctly refuses to widen removal, and Slice 3's deliberate absence is right. But the defect it diagnoses — the reaper's silence — was fixed fifteen days ago, and the fix is the commit the plan quotes.
