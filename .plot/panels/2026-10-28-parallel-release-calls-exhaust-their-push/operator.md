## (a) Problem statement

The facts the plan states are right, but the cause it names is the wrong one, and one number does not add up.

1. **The lock does not cover the part that goes stale.** `plot-push-main.sh:109` takes the lock, and only `git push` runs inside it (`:120-122`). The base is cut before that, outside the lock: `deliver.ts:835` fetches, `:840` cuts `tmpwt` from `origin/main`, `:847` writes the release, and `ladder.ts:173` commits. On a retry, `ladder.ts:196-206` fetches, resets and reapplies, also outside the lock. A writer that waits in the lock queue therefore always holds a base that is older than every push made while it waited. The lock serialises the pushes and makes each one fail fast. It does not make any of them current. The plan's sentence "5 retries do not cover a queue of about 50 writers" describes the symptom. The cause is the scope of the critical section. The `plot-push-main.sh:36-37` comment ("`locked` ... the base is still current") is false for the same reason.
2. **No job ran into the 120 s lock bound.** `grep "push: locked"` over every `plot-*.log` in the desk root returns 0 lines. Every rejection in the surviving release logs is `! [rejected] ... (non-fast-forward)`. The re-run logs (07:08 to 07:13, 52 files) still carry 39 rejections, one plan needed 4 of its 5 attempts (`plot-release-recording-a-slice-pr-number-writes.log`), and none says `locked`. The first-pass logs are gone, because `release.ts:167` truncates the log on each POST.
3. **Simulation (executed).** 20 parallel writers with today's lock: 4 of 20 `FAILED after 5 (locked=0)`, and the other 16 needed 1 to 4 rejections each. The same 20 writers with the lock held from fetch to push: 20 of 20 `ok after 0 rejections`, 5.2 s wall clock. So the defect reproduces at 20 writers, not only at 53, and widening the lock removes it completely.
4. **53 − 11 − 24 = 18 jobs are not accounted for.** The plan says 11 released and 24 failed on push. It does not say what the other 18 did. One candidate the plan should rule in or out: `trees-git.ts:457` runs `git worktree add -B <bookbr> <path> origin/main`, which sets upstream tracking and so writes `.git/config`. In the simulation, parallel `worktree add` calls printed `error: could not lock config file .git/config: File exists` 17 times, and 2 calls exited non-zero. In `deliver.ts:841-843` a non-zero exit becomes the refusal "could not prepare a booking worktree ... Most often origin/main is not fetched", which names the wrong cause. The other candidates are ordinary refusals (docs plans, tag mismatch) and jobs still in their retry loop when someone counted. The plan should name which.
5. Minor: `release.ts` names `releaseStatus` correctly, but the GET route is `index.ts:766`, and no board UI calls `POST /api/release` (no match in `packages/board/src/app`). So `SKILL.md:451` "the route the board's own button calls" is false today. The only caller is the skill, which is the cutter.

## (b) Approach

- **Candidate 1 (serialise in the board) fixes only one of seven writers in one of three processes.** `commitAndPush` has seven callers: `deliver.ts:499` (deliver), `deliver.ts:866` (release), `approve.ts:1270`, `verify.ts:782`, `needs-design.ts:471`, `brief-book.ts:179` and `record-pr.ts:129`. Fleetd runs record-pr and brief-book. The no-board bundles `plot-deliver.mjs`/`plot-approve.mjs` run outside the board. A board-side queue for `/api/release` alone does not order a release job against a fleetd PR record or a parallel approve, and those race in the same way. It also adds new state that a board restart loses. If the queued job's log is opened at POST time, as `release.ts:167` does now, `releaseStatus` (`release.ts:96-97`) reports `running` for ever after a restart, because it infers `running` from the log file existing.
- **The fix belongs in the ladder, which is the script layer.** It is a fifth candidate, and the plan does not list it: `commitAndPush` takes the push lock before its fetch, resets and reapplies under the lock on every attempt (including the first, against a fresh `origin/main`), commits, pushes, and releases the lock. `plot-push-main.sh` then has to accept a lock its caller already holds. One way is an env var naming the holder pid, which `take` treats as held. Without that, the nested `take` waits 120 s and exits 6. The lock lives in the common git dir (`plot-push-lock.mjs:32-33`), so this one change orders the board, fleetd and the CLI bundles together. It follows the layering rule: the ladder is in the board's `entry/` and already reaches the world only through `scripts`/`trees`. Note the cost: the hold time grows from one push to fetch + reapply + commit + push. At about 5 s a hold, 53 writers need more than 120 s. Either the wait must scale (the holder is alive and the queue moves), or a `locked` exit must not spend a ladder attempt. Today `ladder.ts:189-206` counts exit 6 as a rejection and re-cuts.
- **Candidate 2 (more retries)** keeps the race and only makes it less likely. **Candidate 3 (batch push)** breaks the one-plan-per-call controller contract and the per-plan receipt the state gate reads. Reject both.
- **Candidate 4 is needed in a different form.** "Wait between calls" is the wrong instruction once the ladder is fixed: parallel calls become safe. The skill needs a different change: the cutter must read each job's outcome. Step 5b fires N POSTs, gets N `202`s, and then gates on `plot-reconcile-scan.sh | tail -1` (`SKILL.md:500-505`). That gate did its job on 2026-10-28, because it is how the operator learned that 24 plans were still Delivered. But it gives a count, not the reason. "Report what you did NOT mark, with the reason" (`SKILL.md:507-517`) cannot be met without `GET /api/release/<slug>`, which returns `failed` and the last 3 log lines (`release.ts:100-111`). For a push failure those lines are `step: push rejected after 5 attempts` / `plot-deliver: the release is committed on 'plot/release-<slug>' but could not reach main` / `summary: ... push=rejected`, which tell the operator to re-run.

## (c) Slices

- Cut it as two slices, and ship the ladder slice first: it is the bug.
  1. `bug/the-push-lock-covers-the-whole-ladder`: the lock in `commitAndPush` from fetch to push, re-entrant in `plot-push-main.sh`, and `locked` not spent as an attempt. This fixes release, approve, deliver, verify, needs-design, brief-book and record-pr at once. It needs no board queue and no new `releaseStatus` state.
  2. `docs/release-step-5b-reads-each-outcome`: step 5b polls `GET /api/release/<slug>` (or reads each bundle's exit code and `summary:` line) and prints one line per plan with its `failed` message. It also corrects the "board's own button" claim. This slice can merge on its own, before or after slice 1.
- Optional third slice, or a separate bug: `addBranch` with `--no-track` (or `-c branch.autoSetupMerge=false`), so parallel booking worktrees do not contend for `.git/config`. Include it only if the 18 unaccounted jobs turn out to be this refusal.
- Remove candidate 1's board queue from the plan. With slice 1 in place it adds state and restart risk and no extra protection.

## (d) Open questions

- *Which candidate?* None of the four. Choose a fifth: widen the lock in the ladder (b), and add a step 5b outcome read in place of the "wait" text.
- *Does a queued job need a `queued` state?* Not with the ladder fix, because no board queue exists. A job that waits on the lock is `running`, and its log can say `step: waiting for the push lock (held by <pid> <action>)`, so the operator can see why it is slow.
- *Same queue for `/api/approve`?* Yes. The fix must cover it, and the 2026-10-07 measurement (`plot-push-lock.mjs:11-14`) shows it fails in the same way. A fix at the ladder covers approve and the four other writers without extra work, and a board queue does not.
- *Is 120 s the right bound?* For today's code the question does not apply: 0 `locked` lines were found. After the ladder fix it applies, because one hold becomes about 5 s and 53 writers need about 265 s. Make the wait bound follow progress (it restarts whenever the holder changes), or let a `locked` exit wait again without spending an attempt.
- New: what did the 18 jobs that were neither released nor push-rejected do? (a4)
- New: does a sweep also fail where fleetd writes to main during the release? It should, under today's code, which is a further reason the fix cannot live only in `/api/release`.
