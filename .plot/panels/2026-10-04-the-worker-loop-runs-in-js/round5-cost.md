## New findings

1. **No slice says the JS loop appends its endings to `endings.jsonl`, and slice 5's count needs it.** Slice 5 says kinds 1 to 3 come "from `endings.jsonl` and the issues", for the `js` loop as for the baseline. Only the split-out plan writes that file, and it writes it from the shell's `write_ending`. In this plan the `desk` port lists the writes it makes (the ending, the marker, the correction file, the limited record, the seal declaration, the moved worker record, the findings lines), and the `loop-end` write kind ends the loop. Neither names the append, and the append goes to the main checkout, not the desk, so it is not a desk write. A slice-2 implementer who follows the plan builds a `js` loop that leaves `endings.jsonl` empty, and slice 5 finds that out at the end of its window. The fix is one sentence: `loop-end` appends each ending to `endings.jsonl` in the main checkout, as `write_ending` does, best effort.

2. **Slice 5 compares the two loops through different sources for kind 2.** The shell loop writes an ending at six sites in `plot-worker-loop.sh` (`unregistered`, `quiet`, `unreadable`, `bound` twice, `limited`, `unstarted`). It writes none when the CI wait expires: it logs "no CI answer … letting go of the slice" into the desk's log and goes free. The reaper deletes that log. So in the baseline a kind-2 failure appears only when someone files an issue, as #1255 was filed twice. In `js` mode the same event writes a `checks-unanswered` ending, and so does a moved tip, which is by design and not a failure. The `js` side is therefore counted from a fuller source than the shell side, and the flip rule ("the `js` loop's listed failures per slice are no more than the baseline's") compares a complete count with a lower bound. The bias works against `js`, so it cannot flip the default too early, but it can hold the flip on a difference in measurement. Kind 3 ("an exit that held a slice and recorded no reason") has no ending by definition on either side. The plan should name, per kind, the one source both sides are counted from (for kind 2: the issues filed, on both sides; or `checks-unanswered` endings whose detail is not a moved tip, with the baseline then marked unmeasured for kind 2), and say what the flip does with an unmeasured kind.

3. **"One more job per PR fits" counts runs as jobs.** Costs › CI says "the `plot-pm` organisation runs at most 20 jobs at once, and the peak measured on 2026-10-03 was 14". Round 4 measured 14 runs at once, not 14 jobs, and each CI run has two jobs (`corpus`, `validate`). I read the 468 jobs of the 300 runs created from 2026-10-03T20:49Z to 2026-10-04T20:45Z: peak concurrent jobs **21**, at 2026-10-04 13:58:03Z, which is the ceiling. Jobs did queue: 41 of 468 waited more than 60 s. All of the long waits were on `main` pushes: CI `push` `validate` p90 864 s, max 1,077 s; `corpus` max 1,079 s. Each push run has its own concurrency group (`ci.yml`, `group: ci-… github.run_id`), so that wait is the organisation's job limit and not a cancel group. The 15 `pull_request` CI runs with jobs in the window queued 3 s at the median and 39 s at most. So a PR job does not wait today, but the queue is full at the fleet's merge peaks (64 PRs merged on 2026-10-02), and every `loop-js` job is one more job in the queue that a `main` push or a sibling PR waits behind. A PR's queue time counts against the agent's `Checks wait` of 3,600 s, and `validate` alone runs 1,046 s at the median on `pull_request`. The decision can stand, since the repository is public and minutes cost nothing, but the plan should state the measured figure (21 jobs, at the limit) and add one of two limits: a `paths` filter so `loop-js` runs only when a PR touches the loop, its entry, its ports or its tests; or the slice-3 PR records the queue time of its own `loop-js` runs beside the run time. The round-4 amendment had "slice 4's PR records its measured time"; the current plan dropped it.

4. **Memory: only one of the "two monitors" goes.** Costs › Memory says "removing the two monitors saves about 10 MB". Slice 6 removes `plot-build-monitor.sh`, and "What does not change" keeps `plot-agent-monitor.sh`. The shell loop starts no worker monitor today: the live loops' only children are `sleep 60`. Measured on the two live agents (pids 28646 and 31771): wrapper 2.2 MB, agent monitor 6.3 MB, build monitor 6.1 MB, loop 6.9 MB. So slice 6 saves about 6 MB, not 10, and the shell loop holds about 7 MB, not 2 to 5. The net figure moves from +50 to 75 MB to about +51 to 74 MB per agent. No decision changes; the sentence should name the one monitor that goes.

5. **The rate reading in rounds 2 to 4 was wrong, and the budget claim rests on arithmetic only.** `gh api rate_limit` answered "core 0 of 5,000 used" after I had made about 300 REST calls. ## Slice 2 as one deliverable

From the cost side, slice 2 is one deliverable and should not be cut. It ships no caller, so it changes no running cost: no process, no host call and no memory until slice 3 starts the entry. Its CI cost is one real-process test. CI already fills, verifies and starts a systemd unit and tests `KillMode` with the line present and deleted (`ci.yml`, the steps after "The systemd unit is one systemd accepts"), so the `KillMode=process` case has a harness to reuse rather than a new one to build. The contract step it would join runs 374 to 509 s against its 12-minute bound in the 12 runs I read, and `validate` runs at most 1,234 s against 25 minutes, so a process-group test of a few seconds fits. A reviewer checks it by two artefacts: `tsc` refusing a kind without a performer, and the group-stop test. If an approver wants a smaller PR, the cut that keeps each half checkable is `boundedRun` with the group-stop test in one, and `performLoopWrites` with `desk`, `reexec` and `refs.remoteTip` in the other. Neither half has a caller, so the cut changes the review size and nothing else.
