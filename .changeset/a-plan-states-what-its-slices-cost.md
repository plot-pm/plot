A plan now states what its slices cost, summed over the slices measured on this machine and naming the ones that were not. Slices have recorded four token counters since `a-slice-says-what-it-spent` shipped and nothing read them together: `readSliceSpend` answers for one branch, and there was no `planSpend`, no `spendForPlan` and no per-plan sum anywhere in the domain or the board. `planSpend` takes the record's raw lines and the branches a plan names, sums each counter across the `measured` slices, and reports the `absent` and `unreadable` counts **beside** the sum rather than folding them into it. THE SUM IS DELIBERATELY NOT ONE NUMBER. A panel found that a rollup over machine-local records is structurally unsound: reaped desks hold 92.12% of output tokens and 91.94% of cache reads, and a desk is reaped when its work LANDS, so the absences correlate with success — a reader shown *"3 of 5 measured"* reasonably assumes the missing two resemble the three, where on this estate they would be nine tenths of the total. What answers the objection is that the record resolves through `--git-common-dir` and so survives its desk's reap, which makes the gap a COLD START rather than a property: the rollup is complete going forward and empty backward, and that is the sentence a reader needs. One absence stays permanent and is disclosed rather than fixed — `seal_declaration` runs only after `run_bounded` returns 0, so a worker killed by the `Worker bound` never reaches the write site and the most expensive runs record nothing, biasing any rollup LOW in a direction invisible from the records alone. A plan with zero measured slices reports NO total rather than zero, because `reduce(…, 0)` is correct arithmetic and a lie that reports a plan nobody measured as a free one. `absent` and `unreadable` stay two counts and are never collapsed into one, the failure `DeclarationReading`'s docstring records this repo shipping twice, pinned by a fixture holding one of each. No combined fifth total is written, pinned by a key-set assertion on both the counters and the result rather than by prose, since cache reads are 99.36% of a naive four-counter sum and a total would be a cache-read count wearing a cost's name — a helpful `total` beside the four is a two-line change no review would flag. The branches come from the plan file and never from globbing the record, so a record naming an unlisted branch is ignored: one file holds every branch the machine has measured. The record is read ONCE and partitioned rather than re-read per slice. It writes nothing, gates nothing and renders nothing — the render is the named follow-up `a-plan-shows-what-it-cost`, and this is deliberately the first half of a two-slice sequence. **The no-transcript gate is now written rather than claimed**: `workflows/slice-spend.ts` asserted such a test existed and none did, so the delivered slice self-certified in prose while CI stayed green because nothing checked. It is pinned twice, because either half alone is escapable — that the port's `sessions()` is never called, and that no path under a `projects/` transcript directory is opened, watching `node:fs/promises` itself — and verified by mutation: a `record.sessions()` added to `readPlanSpend` fails four of the five. The equivalent claim on the per-slice read is backfilled too.
